## D-001: Where stakeholder needs come from

- **Problem:** The team has no direct access to the planners it serves (LGU planners since D-013). They are the audience for the presentation, not a source of interviews.
- **Decision:** Needs are drawn from published planning documents and checked with the program mentor. Every need is tagged **documented** (cited source), **mentor-validated**, or **assumed**.
- **Reason:** Planners in the audience will know their own policies, so unsupported needs are the easiest thing for them to challenge.
- **Consequence:** A candidate business question that rests mostly on **assumed** needs cannot be approved.

## D-002: No capstone rubric yet

- **Problem:** No official rubric or deadline has been published.
- **Decision:** Build to the course's deliverables: pipeline, model, validation, documentation, Git, a business dashboard, a DQ dashboard, and the Day 9 production practices.
- **Reason:** These are the most complete statement of expectations available today.
- **Consequence:** Scope may shift. **Revisit when the rubric is released.**

## D-003: Higher education is out of scope

- **Problem:** The theme mentions higher-education capacity. Higher education is CHED's mandate, not DepEd's.
- **Decision:** Keep it in scope during source discovery and profiling. The stakeholders are framed as education planners (DepEd and CHED).
- **Reason:** The theme's general question includes it.
- **Consequence:** More sources to profile, and a different grain (one row per institution rather than per school). **Revisit at the end of Phase 3:** keep it only if the profiled data supports it.
- **Revisited 2026-10-05:** higher education is out of scope. Sponsors pointed out that foundational learning gaps in basic education are more urgent, CHED data is available only by region or province, and the stakeholders are now LGU planners (D-013). The CHED source cards stay in the inventory as profiled, but no CHED source moves on to ingestion.

## D-004: Data and secrets stay out of Git

- **Problem:** Raw files, credentials, and large outputs can end up committed by accident, and once pushed they stay in the repository's history.
- **Decision:** No data and no secrets go into Git. Raw, interim, and processed datasets live in the agreed storage; the only exception is small, made-up samples under `tests/data/`. Credentials stay in a local, git-ignored `.env`, and only `.env.example` (names, no values) is committed. CI enforces this in `tests/test_repo_policy.py`.
- **Reason:** The repository is shared by the whole team and reviewed in public pull requests. Checking it automatically on every pull request is more reliable than relying on each person to remember.
- **Consequence:** CI fails a pull request that adds a data file (for example `.csv`, `.xlsx`, or `.zip`), a file over 1 MB, a `.env` file, or text that looks like a token or private key. Generated outputs that are lists of records are written as Markdown, and profiling reads raw files from `RAW_DATA_DIR` outside the repository (D-007).

## D-005: Branching model

- **Problem:** A long-lived `dev` branch alongside `main` is a common pattern, but it adds merge overhead for a small team.
- **Decision:** Use `main` plus short-lived feature branches. `dev` and `prod` exist as Databricks deploy targets, not as branches.
- **Reason:** Separate deploy targets already isolate environments, so a long-lived `dev` branch mostly adds merge overhead.
- **Consequence:** CI runs on PRs into `main`. **Provisional:** the team has not yet confirmed this.

## D-006: Where source documentation lives

- **Problem:** Each source produces an inventory card, profiling findings, and publisher documents (for example DepEd's README files and Technical Notes). These need one predictable home.
- **Decision:** Each source gets a folder, `docs/data/source-inventory/<source_id>/`, with `README.md` (the inventory card) and `profile.md` (findings with evidence). Publisher documents stay with the raw files in raw storage; the card records their URL, size, and SHA-256. Cross-source work stays in `docs/data/cross-source/`.
- **Reason:** Keeping everything about one source together makes review easier. Publisher documents arrive with the data, so they are part of the raw delivery, and recording their checksum proves which version the team relied on.
- **Consequence:** `tests/test_config.py` requires `README.md` for any source past `candidate`, and `profile.md` for any source that is `profiled` or `accepted`.

## D-007: How sources are profiled

- **Problem:** Profiling findings must be reproducible by another engineer, without spending Databricks Free Edition compute on small files.
- **Decision:** Profile locally with DuckDB (pinned in `requirements-dev.txt`), in a `.py` script per source under `analysis/profiling/` with `# %%` cells. Each check prints under the finding ID used in that source's `profile.md`. Scripts read raw data from `RAW_DATA_DIR` and verify file checksums before profiling.
- **Reason:** The DepEd files are about 60,000 rows each, so DuckDB profiles them in seconds on a laptop. DuckDB is in the course's Day 9 tool list. Keeping the code next to the findings lets a reviewer rerun every number.
- **Consequence:** CI cannot run profiling scripts because raw data is not in git. Each PR that changes a profile states that the script was run and the numbers matched. Checks that repeat across sources move into `src/profiling/` once a second source repeats them. The xlsx reader was the first (`src/profiling/xlsx.py`, #33, added 2026-10-01).

## D-008: Develop locally, run on Databricks deliberately

- **Problem:** The team shares one Databricks Free Edition workspace with limited compute. Repeated debugging runs, forgotten sessions, and overlapping full runs could exhaust it before the deadline.
- **Decision:** Profile and develop SQL or PySpark logic on laptops (DuckDB by default; local PySpark optional), then confirm on Databricks once. Full pipeline runs are announced, dashboards get no scheduled refresh during development, and every full run is recorded in `pipeline_runs`. The rules are in `docs/operations/workflow.md`, Part 5.
- **Reason:** The data is small (about 60,000 rows per file), so laptops handle development easily, and each Databricks run is then a deliberate confirmation rather than trial and error.
- **Consequence:** Local and Databricks behavior can differ (SQL dialect, no Unity Catalog locally), so logic is not trusted until it has run on Databricks. Setup is longer: every teammate needs a local Python environment (`docs/getting-started/terminal-setup.md`).

## D-009: Workspace, catalog, and raw storage

- **Problem:** The team needs one shared place for data in Databricks Free Edition, usable by all five teammates, before ingestion starts.
- **Decision:** One workspace (`dbc-76bcfddb-1669`) with CLI profile `reached-hq`. Catalog `edu_access`, created through the UI (Free Edition requires Default Storage, which the CLI could not use). The catalog, schemas, and raw volume are owned by the `reached-hq` group, not a person. Raw files go to the managed volume `` edu_access.`00-source`.raw ``. All five teammates are workspace admins.
- **Reason:** Group ownership means nobody is blocked when one teammate is unavailable. A managed volume needs no credentials, and the data is small (the DepEd downloads are about 3 MB each). Making everyone an admin keeps setup fast for a student team.
- **Consequence:** Every teammate can change or delete anything in the workspace and the catalog, which is the opposite of least privilege (Day 9); the team accepts that for the capstone. **Provisional:** if the mentor approves the course R2 bucket, an R2-backed volume is added and this entry is updated.

## D-010: Using AI

- **Problem:** Teammates use AI tools for code, SQL, and documentation. Nothing records when AI helped, so a reviewer cannot tell which statements were checked by a person. AI can produce a column description, a number, or a platform limit that looks right and is wrong, and it can be given data or credentials that should never leave the team.
- **Decision:** AI help is allowed for all work. Every pull request states whether AI helped and what it did. If it helped, the work must pass four gates (data, AI claims, output, accountability) before the pull request is opened. The rules are in `CONTRIBUTING.md` under **Using AI**, and the pull request template carries an **AI help** block. Issue templates do not: the pull request is where work is reviewed, so that is where AI help is declared.
- **Reason:** Banning AI would be ignored and unverifiable. Disclosure plus checkable gates keeps the author accountable and tells the reviewer where to look. Stating the gates once in `CONTRIBUTING.md` means a change is made in one place, not in the template.
- **Consequence:** CI (the **PR declares AI help** check) fails a pull request whose AI help section is not filled in, but it only sees the ticks: disclosure is still an honor system, and a false "No AI help" cannot be detected. The **Accountability** gate carries the weight: work its author cannot explain in review is sent back. Open pull requests from before this decision add the block when next edited. **Provisional:** revisit once the whole team has agreed to it, and if the course publishes its own AI policy.

## D-011: Interpreting undocumented columns

- **Problem:** Publishers often leave columns undocumented (in PSA's PSGC workbook, 6 of 11 columns). The rule "never write a description from guesswork" keeps the dictionary honest, but it leaves those columns blank even when their meaning is well supported by a law, by the data, or by an obvious header. Teammates then either guess silently in later work or are tempted to write a guess into the publisher's column.
- **Decision:** Add an **Our interpretation** column to every data dictionary. **Description (publisher)** stays the publisher's words only, including documentation given as an image. **Our interpretation** holds the team's reading, and every entry starts with a label, **[other source]**, **[observed]**, or **[assumed]**, followed by its evidence. An [assumed] entry the pipeline relies on needs a suspected finding in `profile.md`. The rules are in `docs/data/source-inventory/template/data_dictionary.md`.
- **Reason:** Separate columns let a reader tell at a glance who said what, which is what planners will question. The labels follow the documented / mentor-validated / assumed tagging from D-001, so the team uses one way of stating how sure it is. Keeping the text in the script keeps the dictionary generated and reproducible (D-007).
- **Consequence:** Existing dictionaries (`deped_enrollment`, `deped_facilities`) gain the column when their script is next changed; DepEd documents almost every column, so they need few entries. Sources in review, starting with `psa_psgc` (#19), add it before approval. Interpretations can be wrong: the label and evidence make that visible, and they are reviewed like any other claim.

## D-012: Which PSGC version to use

- **Problem:** The anchor data is DepEd SY 2023-24, but the PSGC we profiled is 2Q 2026. Between them the Negros Island Region (NIR) was created and Sulu moved from BARMM to Region IX, so region labels and codes differ. The PSA API returns no records for `Q3_2023`; the nearest version it serves, `Q4_2023`, was pulled and compared.
- **Decision:** Use the PSGC 2Q 2026 workbook as the one master reference for all school years. No crosswalk to older versions is built. DepEd files carry place names only, so joins use province, municipality, and barangay names inside their parent, never region. For SY 2023-24 results by region, use DepEd's own region column or report at province level.
- **Reason:** About 1,855 of about 43,760 codes differ from `Q4_2023`, almost all from NIR and Sulu, and name joins are not affected by a code change. `Q4_2023` has 457 names with broken accents and no 2024 population for about 1,780 barangays. 2Q 2026 has neither problem, is PSA's published file, and is already profiled.
- **Consequence:** Region from PSGC must not be used for Negros or Sulu in SY 2023-24 results. Barangays split or renamed since 2023 (for example Barangay 176 in Caloocan) are flagged as unmatched or ambiguous, not forced. **Revisit** if a source arrives with codes from another PSGC version, or if the team needs code-level history; then build a crosswalk from `Correspondence Code`. Revisited on 2026-10-09 for poverty and HDX, which carry old codes: D-030 allows joins through `Correspondence Code` under conditions; PSGC 2Q 2026 stays the master. Revisited on 2026-10-16 for DepEd schools: D-049 takes each school's barangay code from the EMISD workbook, so DepEd joins no longer rely on names alone; PSGC 2Q 2026 stays the master. Evidence and matching rules: [psa_psgc README](../data/source-inventory/psa_psgc/README.md#version-used-and-sy-2023-24).

## D-013: Who the stakeholders are

- **Problem:** The project was framed for DepEd and CHED planners. Sponsors asked what a mayor or local planner would actually need, for example whether a mayor can zoom into a barangay and trust what they see.
- **Decision:** The stakeholders are LGU planners, with students as the focus. This decision originally proposed analysis at city/municipality and barangay level; D-042 later fixed city/municipality as the approved analytical grain and kept barangay as an AP1 roll-up, a drill-down, and a validation aid.

- **Consequence:** Planner needs are drawn from LGU-facing documents (D-001). Barangays are never ranked.

## D-014: How a raw delivery is identified and approved

- **Problem:** DepEd publishes each school year as a new zip, and re-uploads years it has already published (the enrollment files were re-uploaded on 2025-10-15). The files have no `updated_at` or other change field. A pipeline that trusts file names would load a changed file as if it were the old one, or load the same file twice under different names.
- **Decision:** A delivery is identified by the SHA-256 of the archive, and its rows by the SHA-256 of the data file inside it. Each source has a contract, `config/ingestion/<source_id>.json`, listing the schema versions (exact, ordered column lists) and every approved delivery with its checksums, encoding, schema version, and row count. A delivery is approved by merging its entry in a reviewed pull request; the pipeline never approves anything. A header that matches no schema version stops the load; a new version is added to the contract in a pull request, and Bronze gains the new columns from the contract, never from a file.
- **Reason:** A checksum is the only change signal these files carry, and it cannot be faked by a rename. Approval in a pull request puts a reviewer between a new file and the tables, which is where the encoding and schema changes of SY 2025-26 were caught (enrollment profile O-6 to O-8).
- **Consequence:** A file the contract does not list is recorded as `blocked`; a file with an approved name but a different checksum is `blocked` as a possible revision; a re-zipped copy of an approved data file is `skipped`. Each new school year needs a profile update and a contract entry before it loads. `tests/test_ingestion_validate.py` fails if the contract's checksums and row counts are not also on the source card. **Provisional:** the team has not yet reviewed it (#11).

## D-015: Bronze keeps every delivery and every version

- **Problem:** When a publisher revises a school year it has already published, the team must choose between replacing the earlier rows and keeping both. There is no evidence yet of what DepEd's re-uploads change. The pipeline also needs a way to load the same file again without duplicating rows, while keeping rows the publisher itself repeated.
- **Decision:** Bronze is append-only, one table per source covering all school years. A revised file for a school year is approved as the next `delivery_version` with `supersedes` naming the earlier one, and both stay in Bronze. A Bronze row is identified by `(source_sha256, source_row_number)` and loaded with an insert-only MERGE, so a rerun inserts nothing and publisher duplicates (different row numbers) are all kept and flagged as WARN. Because Delta commits one table at a time, the batch's `succeeded` status in `01-control`.ingestion_batches is written last and acts as the commit marker. Downstream reads `01-control`.current_batches: for each school year, the latest succeeded version.
- **Reason:** Keeping both versions is reversible and choosing one is not; the history is what lets the team answer "what changed?" when a number moves. The data-file checksum, rather than the archive's, makes a re-zipped copy load nothing; a hash of row content would wrongly merge publisher duplicates.
- **Consequence:** A revised file that changes one value reloads every row of that school year as a new version (about 60,000 rows), which is acceptable at this size. A load that dies after its MERGE leaves rows that downstream cannot see until a retry reconciles them. **Open:** whether the latest version should always be current, or whether some revisions need review first (decide when the first real revision arrives). **Provisional:** the team has not yet reviewed it (#11).

## D-016: Raw storage layout

- **Problem:** Raw files are stored flat, one folder per publisher with original file names; the DepEd files were uploaded that way to `/Volumes/edu_access/00-source/raw/deped/` on 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-03. A later download with the same name, such as a DepEd re-upload, would overwrite the earlier file, and the earlier bytes would be gone.
- **Decision:** Files already in `raw/<publisher>/` stay where they are. No raw file is ever overwritten: any later download, whether a new school year or a re-download of a published one, goes into `raw/<publisher>/<download date, YYYY-MM-DD>/` under its original file name. Every folder has a `SHA256SUMS.txt` covering all of its files, updated in the same upload.
- **Reason:** Moving the uploaded files would change paths teammates already use, for no gain: ingestion searches the publisher folder at any depth and identifies files by checksum (D-014), not by path. What protects history is the no-overwrite rule, and a dated subfolder is enough to keep it.
- **Consequence:** The folder mixes the first downloads with dated later ones; a dated folder also records when its files were retrieved. Before an upload, list the target folder to check that no file of that name is there. The flat uploads do not meet #10's "one folder per source"; whether per-publisher folders are enough is open. `SHA256SUMS.txt` in `raw/deped/` does not yet cover the files added on 2026-10-03 (#10). Local copies may keep the `raw-data/<publisher>/original/` layout used for profiling. **Revised 2026-10-06:** first proposed one folder per source and download date for every file; changed after finding the files already uploaded flat. **Provisional:** the team has not yet reviewed it. **Revised 2026-10-16:** a file downloaded before its delivery is approved waits in `raw/_holding/<publisher>/<download date>/`, outside every landing folder, because the pipeline blocks an unapproved delivery it finds in `raw/<publisher>/` and fails the run; it moves to `raw/<publisher>/<download date>/` when the contract pull request merges (first used for SY 2020-21 enrollment, #160).

## D-017: DuckDB stands in for Delta in local ingestion runs

- **Problem:** Ingestion logic must be tested on every pull request without Databricks credentials (D-008), but local tests can only prove what the local engine can show.
- **Decision:** Local runs and CI use DuckDB through a thin store (`src/ingestion/store.py`) that runs the same `etl/` SQL files as Databricks, translating backticks, `STRING`, and Delta table properties. Writes are single statements, never a multi-table transaction, to match Delta. Spark/Delta is the store on Databricks.
- **Reason:** DuckDB is already pinned and used by the team (D-007), supports MERGE, and loads all three enrollment years (about 180,000 rows) in about seven seconds. Local PySpark with Delta needs Java on every laptop and still has no Unity Catalog.
- **Consequence:** Three Databricks runtime differences are imitated in `tests/test_databricks_runtime.py` (no `__file__` under `exec`, `SystemExit(0)` reported as failure, lazy `SELECT`). Four behaviors are not proven locally and must be confirmed on Databricks: Delta column mapping for the column name with spaces, single-table commit semantics under a real failure, Unity Catalog permissions, and reading raw files from `/Volumes/` paths. Found on the first Databricks run (2026-10-06): a task needs `source: GIT` to read its file from the repository; tables are owned by whoever ran the job unless handed to the group (now done in every `CREATE` file); and the first run took 39 minutes against 7 seconds locally, 35.5 of them waiting for Free Edition serverless capacity that other compute was using (the work itself took 3.5; each small control-table write takes 3 to 13 seconds). Confirmed by that run: column mapping, reading from `/Volumes/`, and Unity Catalog permissions once objects belong to the group; single-table commits under a real failure remain untested. **Provisional:** revisit if the Databricks confirmation run finds a behavior that differs.

## D-018: Workbook deliveries that cover several estimate years

- **Problem:** `psa_poverty_stat` arrives as one xlsx workbook whose wide rows hold 2018, 2021 and 2023 estimates together, under a five-row merged header, with region banner rows and a footer. The DepEd design (D-014, D-015) assumes a zip with one CSV and one school year per file, and counts versions per school year. Giving each wide row one year, or splitting the workbook into three "deliveries", would misstate what PSA delivered. The workbook has no `updated_at`.
- **Decision:** Add a second delivery format, `xlsx_sheet`, to the same pipeline. A workbook is identified by its SHA-256 (D-014) and approved in `config/ingestion/psa_poverty_stat.json` with its sheet, estimate years, and profiled row counts (body, units, banners, footer). Its layout (header rows, the merged groups, first data row, the `Notes:` footer marker, the ignored empty column S) is part of the schema version, so any undocumented change stops the batch. Versions are counted per `logical_dataset`, and two datasets may not share an estimate year: a workbook that republishes an approved year can only be approved as the next `delivery_version` of that dataset, with `supersedes`, and a revision must keep every estimate year of the version it supersedes (it may add years), so the one current version per dataset never hides a year. The estimate years are batch metadata (`estimate_years_covered`), not a column value of any row. Bronze keeps every row of the sheet's used range (title, header, body, footer, each labelled by `source_row_kind`, so no worksheet row is dropped) and every cell as stored text, identified by `(source_sha256, source_row_number)` with the Excel row number, and carry `source_sheet`. The used range (A1:S1641) is pinned in the contract. Known publisher issues (O-2 to O-9) are WARN results with their profiled counts, never corrections. `ingestion_batches` gains `logical_dataset`, `estimate_years_covered` and `source_sheet`, `ingestion_batch_attempts` gains the first two (nullable, added if missing), and `current_batches` takes the latest version per `COALESCE(school_year, logical_dataset)`.
- **Reason:** Keeping one pipeline means the identity, idempotency, control tables, gate, and Databricks job already verified for DepEd apply unchanged; only reading and period logic differ (`src/ingestion/workbook.py`, `src/ingestion/formats.py`). Forbidding overlap across datasets makes "the same year delivered twice" a reviewer's decision instead of a silent second copy. The Excel row number lets anyone open the workbook at the exact line a Bronze row came from.
- **Consequence:** Additive columns on shared control tables: existing rows keep their meaning (NULL for school-year sources), but the first Databricks run after this change alters shared tables and must be announced; `ALTER TABLE ADD COLUMN` on Delta is confirmed only by that run. The DepEd Bronze DDL and behavior are unchanged (tests). **Open:** whether a later PSA release that adds a year and revises earlier ones is a revision of the existing dataset or a new dataset (decide when it arrives; the contract forces the choice). Merged-header, used-range and footer checks were confirmed by a local run on the real workbook on 2026-10-07 (1,641 rows, every known-issue count equal to the profile). **Provisional:** the team has not yet reviewed it.

## D-019: PSGC workbooks, one per quarter

- **Problem:** `psa_psgc` arrives as one xlsx workbook per publication quarter, with no outer zip, the data on sheet `PSGC` under a single header row, and a quarter rather than a school year. The pipeline (D-014, D-015) assumed a zip with one CSV per school year. The header has line breaks, an unnamed column J, and version-specific text (the DOF order, the census). 84 codes are stored as numbers in the workbook, and population uses an accounting format that displays 0 as `-`. PSA replaces the file at a stable URL, and the workbook has no `updated_at`.
- **Decision:** Add a third delivery format, `xlsx_table` (`src/ingestion/xlsx_table.py`), next to `zip_csv` and `xlsx_sheet` (D-018), as one more class in `src/ingestion/formats.py`; the format is chosen by `format` in `config/ingestion/psa_psgc.json`, and every sheet is opened by the shared `workbook.open_sheet`. A workbook is identified by its SHA-256 and approved in the contract (D-014). Its quarter is read from the file name and must equal the quarter of the `Metadata` publication date and the approval. The header must equal a schema version's exact `source_headers`, each mapped to a fixed snake_case Bronze column; a changed DOF order or census header stops the batch until a new schema version is added. Cells are kept as stored text; numbers keep their stored digits, not Excel's display; a code stored as a number is accepted only if it is already 10 digits, and nothing is padded. A Bronze row is identified by (workbook SHA-256, Excel row number), never by the PSGC code, and every quarter and version is kept. The control tables' existing period column, `school_year`, holds the quarter (`2026-Q2`) for this source by documented convention; Bronze names it `publication_period`. Of D-018's columns, `source_sheet` is `PSGC` and `logical_dataset` and `estimate_years_covered` are NULL; `current_batches` partitions by `COALESCE(school_year, logical_dataset)`, so it keeps one current version per quarter. The master reference quarter (D-012) is `master_reference_period` in the contract, changed only by a reviewed pull request. Shared changes are small and backward compatible: the format registered in `contract.py` and `formats.py`; an opt-in `require_approved_deliveries` flag in `pipeline.py` (PSGC: a missing approved workbook fails the run); and in `workbook.open_sheet`, the refusal of macro-enabled workbooks and of XML parts that are not UTF-8, which now protects both workbook formats.
- **Reason:** One pipeline keeps identity, idempotency, the control tables, the gate and the Databricks job that are already verified for DepEd. Quarters sort like school years, so storing the quarter in `school_year` makes `current_batches`, backfill detection and existing queries work with no change to shared tables, and works alongside the columns D-018 added. Keying on the code would turn Bronze into a latest-state table and erase the quarterly history that D-012 relies on. Keeping stored digits avoids turning a population of 0 into the publisher's `-` for unknown.
- **Consequence:** The `school_year` column name is misleading for PSGC rows; queries filter it by `source_id`. A re-issued quarter reloads all 43,768 rows as a new version. Data-quality results against the workbook's own `National Summary`, the code hierarchy and the profiled counts are WARN, not blocking; a profiled characteristic is PASS at its profiled count and WARN only when the count changes. A duplicate `psgc_code` is kept in Bronze and recorded, and Silver must stop on it, because PSGC is what Integration joins to. Confirmed locally on the real 2Q 2026 workbook on 2026-10-07 (43,768 rows, every count equal to the source card, 0 FAIL, then a skip), and on Databricks `dev` on 2026-10-08 at commit `cb274bf` (same counts, then a skip). That run found the shared `dev` control tables already carrying D-018's columns before #109 merged; with #109 merged, `main` creates and fills those columns itself. After merging `main`, the local and Databricks runs were repeated at the merged commit (see the evidence files). **Open:** a generic period column if Ina prefers one; whether any data-quality result should block. **Provisional:** the team has not yet reviewed it (#82).

## D-020: Every SQL file is its own job task

- **Problem:** The job ran each source as one Python task that loaded the files and then ran the Bronze gate SQL inside it. The SQL the pipeline depends on was invisible in the job: a failed gate showed as a failed Python task, and the job did not show the planned layer-by-layer flow (source check → Bronze → Bronze gate → Silver → Silver gate).
- **Decision:** One job, `edu_access_pipeline` (renamed from `bronze_ingest`, because it runs more than Bronze). Python runs only what SQL cannot: checking and loading the deliveries, which are zipped CSVs with a declared encoding or Excel workbooks (D-018, D-019) (`ingest --no-gate --no-setup`). Every SQL file in `etl/` that a run executes is its own `sql_task` on the SQL warehouse, read from the deployed commit. The idempotent control DDL runs once in order, with one Python task, `add_control_columns` (`cli.py columns`), between the control tables and `05_create_current_batches`, then every source raw-table DDL fans out from the completed control setup; `--no-setup` prevents the parallel Python loaders from repeating that DDL. Source lanes never depend on one another; inside a lane, raw-table creation, ingestion, Bronze validation, Silver cleaning, and Silver validation run in order with `ALL_SUCCESS`. Control validation is the source-lane fan-in. Mapping tasks run in parallel before Integration, dimensions run in parallel before facts, facts run in parallel before Analytics, and all analytical-problem outputs run in parallel. SQL tasks get `:code_revision` and `:run_id` (`{{job.run_id}}`) from job parameters. `python -m src.job.local_run` executes a deterministic topological order on DuckDB, and `tests/test_local_job.py` does so in CI. Revised by Ina on 2026-10-08 (#47).
- **Reason:** Each check is then its own task with its own status and log, the SQL that reviewers read is the SQL that runs, and the job follows the planned flow. The local runner keeps D-008 and D-017: the job's order and SQL are tested before Databricks.
- **Consequence:** Every run starts the SQL warehouse as well as serverless job compute, which on Free Edition share limited capacity: leave the warehouse stopped and notebooks detached before a run. A Bronze load's `pipeline_runs` row covers the load only, so it can say `succeeded` when its gate task failed; the gate's verdict is its task's status and its rows in `data_quality_results`, under the job's run id. To link the two, each load passes `--job-run-id {{job.parameters.run_id}}` and stores it in `pipeline_runs.job_run_id` (NULL for manual runs, whose gate writes under their own `run_id`): a load's true outcome is `pipeline_runs` joined to `data_quality_results` on `q.run_id IN (r.run_id, r.job_run_id) AND q.source_id = r.source_id`: the load's own batch checks are under its `run_id`, the gate task's under `job_run_id` (query in [ingestion.md](../operations/ingestion.md#running-on-databricks)). It is a separate column, not the load's `run_id`, because every lane of one job run shares the job run id and a load's row is keyed by its own `run_id`. A Silver build is a job task, not a load: its `silver_build` row takes the job run id as `run_id`, one row per source, keyed by `run_id`, `pipeline_name`, and `source_id` (D-025), so a query about loads filters `pipeline_name = 'bronze_ingest'`. Its `job_run_id` is the same job run id, so one filter on `job_run_id` finds every row of a job run, Bronze loads and Silver builds alike. `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` adds no column to an existing table and Databricks has no `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`, so columns added after a control table was created (`control.ADDED_COLUMNS`: D-018's and `job_run_id`) are added by `add_control_columns`, once, before the view that reads them is replaced. Without it the view task fails on control tables created before #109, every lane is skipped, and rerunning cannot fix it (found in the #113 review). `ingest` without `--no-gate` still runs the gate itself, so a task added before this change keeps working until it gets its own gate task. Renaming the job key replaced the deployed dev job: runs before 2026-10-08 are under `[dev <user>] bronze_ingest`. **Revisit** if the warehouse's start-up or capacity makes runs too slow on Free Edition.

## D-021: Silver keeps the source's grain and shape

- **Problem:** DepEd enrollment arrives as one row per school per school year with 66 count columns (grade × sex, and strand for senior high). Silver could reshape it into one row per school, year, grade, sex, and strand, which most charts need, or keep the publisher's shape.
- **Decision:** Silver keeps the grain (one row per school per school year, key `school_id` + `school_year`) and the wide shape. Only types, names, labels, and missing values change. Unpivoting is a Gold modelling decision. Approved by Ina on 2026-10-07 (#85).
- **Reason:** The architecture defines Silver as "same grain". Keeping the shape means every Silver count is exactly one published cell, so reconciliation with Bronze is a column-by-column sum, and the planner-facing model can still be chosen once the business question is approved.

## D-022: Category labels are mapped through reviewed configuration

- **Problem:** DepEd relabels categories between years (`DepEd` → `DepED Managed`, `True` → `Yes`, `Standalone School` → `School with no Annexes`, a trailing space on `Non-Sectarian `; profile O-8, O-9). A CASE expression scattered through SQL hides those choices from review, and a new label in a future year could be accepted silently.
- **Decision:** Every accepted label of `region`, `legislative_district`, `sector`, `school_management`, `annex_status`, the three `offers_*` booleans, and `modified_curricular_offering_classification` is listed in `config/mappings/deped_enrollment.json`, with the standard value it maps to and its evidence (how many of the same `school_id`s carried the old and the new label). Labels are compared after whitespace is normalized. The Silver SQL is generated from that file. A label that is not listed becomes NULL and fails the gate (`labels_mapped_<column>`). The source owner (@hyenalouise for this source) approves a new label in a pull request to the file. Approved by Ina on 2026-10-07 (#85).
- **Reason:** The mapping is then one reviewed file with evidence per label, like the Bronze contract (D-014). Failing on an unknown label follows the lecture rule that unexpected data is never ignored, and matches how Bronze treats an unknown header.
- **Consequence:** A new school year with a new label fails Silver until the mapping is updated and `etl/03_silver/` regenerated; meanwhile the last build that passed stays published and the failed one waits in the candidate tables for review (D-025). The standard sets are the SY 2020-21, 2023-24, and 2024-25 labels (three of four years), because DepEd's README matches no year exactly. SY 2020-21 publishes one label no later year uses, `school_management = Local Government` (154 public schools, profile O-8); it is kept as its own standard value, not mapped to `DepEd`, which would claim a management the file does not publish for that year, nor to `LUC`, which is a SUC/LUC-sector label. Facilities and personnel share the enrollment `school_management` set, so the value is listed there too. Gold filters public schools on `sector`, not on `school_management = 'DepEd'`. Approved by Ina on 2026-10-18 (#171). `region` is gated but kept as published: region comparability (NIR) is Integration's and Gold's concern (D-012).

## D-023: Silver never invents a value

- **Problem:** Blank cells, columns a year does not publish, placeholder addresses, mangled characters, and inconsistent spacing all need a rule, and each rule can hide information if it is applied silently.
- **Decision:**
  - A blank count stays NULL, never 0. In SY 2025-26 DepEd leaves counts blank where earlier years published 0 (for example the senior-high columns of every school with `offers_shs = No`), so NULL in Silver means "not published".
  - A column absent from a year's file (`g11_sshs_*` and the curricular classification before SY 2025-26) is NULL too, and `schema_version` tells it apart from a published blank. No extra flag columns. Approved by Ina on 2026-10-07.
  - Counts are cast to INT only when they are one to nine digits and at most the reviewed plausible maximum (10,000); anything else quarantines the row (D-024). Every sum of counts is taken in BIGINT, so nine-digit values cannot overflow it. `TRY_CAST` alone is not used, because DuckDB casts `'5.0'` to 5 while Spark returns NULL.
  - Text is trimmed, runs of spaces (and tabs and no-break spaces) become one space, and blank becomes NULL. Place names are not otherwise changed: `(Capital)` and every other spelling stay for Integration (D-012).
  - Two exact character repairs are made in free text: `Ã‘` → `Ñ` and `Ã±` → `ñ` (UTF-8 read as Windows-1252, present in every year as published; O-17). Approved by Ina on 2026-10-07 for `Ã‘`; `Ã±` is the lowercase form of the same error, behind the 11 school names the profile counts under O-17, and is provisional until the team confirms it.
  - `street_address` values `-`, `n/a`, and `N/A` become NULL (O-14).
- **Reason:** "Make missingness explicit; prefer NULL; never invent a value" (lectures, Day 4). Bronze keeps every original value, so each rule can be checked and undone by reading Bronze, and each rule's affected rows are recorded per run in `data_quality_results` (`rule_*`).
- **Consequence:** Gold must not read NULL as zero, and must decide per measure how to treat a level a school does not publish. Integration matches the repaired names; the published bytes stay in Bronze. Other address placeholders (`none`, `not applicable`, `NA`) are left as published until the team extends the list.

## D-024: Quarantine what cannot be trusted; flag the rest

- **Problem:** Some rows cannot be used as published (a negative count, a repeated school), others are valid but need care (overseas schools placed in Pasig City, schools with no learners, barangay names cut at 40 characters). Deleting either kind loses evidence; keeping all of them silently misleads.
- **Decision:** A row is quarantined, with every reason that applies, when `school_id` is blank or not six digits, when a `school_id` appears twice in a school year (every copy), or when a count is negative, not a whole number, or above the plausible maximum in the mapping (`count_columns.plausible_max`, 10,000). It moves to `deped_enrollment_quarantine` with its Bronze lineage and is never deleted. Everything else stays in `deped_enrollment_clean`, flagged where needed: `is_overseas`, `enrollment_status` (`has_learners`, `all_zero`, `no_counts`), `barangay_possibly_truncated`, and a NULL `barangay` for a blank one. Any quarantined row is WARN; more than 1% of a school year's Bronze rows is FAIL. Approved by Ina on 2026-10-07 (#43, #85); the plausible maximum approved by Ina on 2026-10-09 (#85).
- **Reason:** Detect, flag, quarantine, fail (lectures, Day 4): a quarantine keeps the row reviewable without letting it into totals, and a threshold stops a broken delivery from passing as a small loss. No row of the three acquired years is quarantined (profile O-1, O-2), so 1% leaves room for a few bad rows without accepting a broken file. Profile O-2 asks for a range check: of 9,260,590 published counts the largest is 4,097 and none exceed 5,000, so 10,000 never quarantines a published value and catches a count with an extra digit, which no reconciliation can see because Bronze holds the same value.
- **Consequence:** Every current Bronze row is in exactly one Silver table, checked per school year (`rows_reconcile`). Flags are never NULL, so `WHERE NOT is_overseas` is safe; nullable values such as `offers_shs` need `IS NOT TRUE`. A school whose two rows conflict leaves the clean table entirely until the publisher or the team resolves it. The maximum catches an impossible size, not an implausible change: the suspected Grade 11 jumps at three schools in Maguindanao stay in Silver (profile S-4, [limitations](limitations.md)). **Revisit** the 1% threshold if a real delivery quarantines rows, and the maximum if a published count approaches it.

## D-025: Silver runs after its Bronze gate, rebuilds on every run, and publishes only a build that passes

- **Problem:** Free Edition's serverless capacity is shared and limited: in the Bronze runs most of the time was spent waiting for compute, and each small control-table statement took 3 to 13 seconds. Silver must be repeatable and safe to rerun, must never build on a failed Bronze load, and should not spend compute it does not need.
- **Decision:**
  - Each source's Silver is two SQL tasks in the job (D-020): `NN_clean_<source>` right after the source's Bronze gate, then `90_validate_<table>`, each only if the task before it succeeded.
  - Silver reads only Bronze rows whose batch is in `current_batches`: after a publisher revision the latest delivery version is current (D-015 default).
  - Every run rebuilds the source's two **candidate** tables (`<table>_candidate`, `<quarantine>_candidate`, registered in `config/tables.yml`) with `CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE ... AS SELECT`, and records the run as `running` in `pipeline_runs`. The build never writes the Silver tables.
  - The gate checks the candidates, counting only its own execution's results. On any FAIL it records the run `failed` and stops the task before publishing, so the Silver tables keep the last build that passed. Otherwise it copies the candidate quarantine to the Silver quarantine, then the candidate clean to the Silver clean table, and only then records the run `succeeded`.
  - Gold reads a Silver table only when the `run_id` its rows carry is a `silver_build` run that `succeeded`; a later failed run does not take the last good build away.
  - Approved by Ina on 2026-10-07 with a skip when Bronze is unchanged (#43); revised on 2026-10-08 to rebuild every run (option 1A), when Silver became SQL tasks; revised on 2026-10-09 to publish only after the gate passes (#119), because a failed build had replaced every school year of the last good one.
- **Reason:** About 180,000 rows rebuild in about a second, so a full rebuild is simpler than a MERGE keyed on Bronze rows or a per-year replace, is atomic per table, and gives the same result however often it runs. A SQL file cannot decide to skip itself; keeping the skip would need two more tasks per source and a condition task not yet tried on Free Edition, to save that second, while the warehouse is already running for the Bronze gate.
- **Consequence:** A revised delivery replaces its school year because `current_batches` moves to the new version, once its build passes. Every run spends the rebuild and writes a new version of both candidate tables, and a run that passes also writes a copy of each to the Silver tables. A failed build stays in the candidate tables until the next run, for review. The two Silver tables are two Delta commits: a task that dies between them leaves its run `running`, the Silver quarantine from the new build and the Silver clean table from the last good one, so Gold, which trusts each table by its `run_id`, still reads the last good clean table. **Revisit** when a source grows past what a full rebuild handles cheaply, or if warehouse time becomes the limit.

## D-026: GeoJSON boundary files

- **Problem:** `hdx_boundaries` arrives as one GeoJSON FeatureCollection, `phl_admin3.geojson` (555 MB, 1,642 cities and municipalities), not a zip with a CSV (D-014) or a workbook (D-018, D-019). Each feature carries 31 properties and a polygon of up to 11.7 MB of text. The file's period is a boundary reference date (`valid_on = 2025-02-13`), not a school year, and its name holds no date. Parsing the geometry on the way in would mean choosing a spatial library, a precision, and a validity rule before anyone has decided them, and any of those can change a shape silently.
- **Decision:** Add a fourth delivery format, `geojson_features`, to the same pipeline (`src/ingestion/geojson_features.py`, and `GeojsonFeatures` in `src/ingestion/formats.py`). The file is its own delivery: it is identified by its SHA-256 (D-014), and the contract's `archive` and `data_member` name the same file, with the same checksum. Each feature is one Bronze row, identified by `(source_sha256, source_row_number)` with the feature's position in the file. Every property is kept as text exactly as written in the file (numbers keep the file's digits, `true`/`false` stay text, JSON `null` becomes NULL; nested values are refused), and the geometry is kept as the raw GeoJSON text in the last column, `geometry`: never parsed, simplified, reprojected, or repaired in Bronze. The delivery's period is its reference date, stored in the control tables' `school_year` column (as D-019 does for the PSGC quarter), and every feature's `valid_on` must equal it. A missing geometry, another reference date, or a non-WGS 84 CRS fails the batch; a geometry type other than Polygon or MultiPolygon is a WARN. Rows are merged in chunks of at most `max_stage_bytes` (64 MB), each its own MERGE.
- **Reason:** One pipeline keeps the identity, idempotency, control tables, and gate already verified for DepEd and PSA. Keeping geometry as text keeps Bronze a faithful copy of the delivery: Silver can parse it with whatever the team chooses and always check the result against the raw file. Reusing `school_year` for the date avoids a schema change to shared control tables. Chunks keep one staged batch small enough for Spark Connect when single polygons are megabytes.
- **Consequence:** Silver must parse geometry into a spatial type and validate it, recording any repair (superseded by D-040: Silver keeps the geometry as the published text, checks every shape, and repairs nothing). `school_year` holds a date for this source, so anything reading it must not assume a school year; renaming it to a generic `period` is open (also D-019). Only ADM3 is ingested; ADM4 would be its own contract and table. A local run on the real file on 2026-10-08 loaded 1,642 rows with no WARN or FAIL, and a second run skipped it ([evidence](../../evidence/pipeline-runs/2026-10-08-hdx-boundaries-local-idempotency.md)). Confirmed on Databricks `dev` on 2026-10-08 at commit `67ea4a2`: the same 1,642 rows, then two skips, so serverless handles the file's memory and the 64 MB chunks ([evidence](../../evidence/pipeline-runs/2026-10-08-hdx-boundaries-databricks-idempotency.md)). **Provisional:** the team has not yet reviewed it.

## D-027: Poverty 2023 beside SY 2023-24

- **Problem:** The 2023 small-area poverty estimates use the 2023 FIES and the January 2024 LFS. SY 2023-24 runs across 2023 and 2024. The two overlap but are not the same period ([time alignment](../data/cross-source/coverage.md#time-alignment-with-the-sy-2023-24-anchor)).

## D-029: One set of match columns for every crosswalk

- **Problem:** DepEd, barangay population, CMCI, poverty, and HDX each reach PSGC 2Q 2026 a different way: by name inside the parent, by code, by `Correspondence Code`, or by review. If each mapping table records this its own way, match rates cannot be compared and a reviewer cannot audit them the same way.
- **Decision:** Every mapping table to PSGC in Integration (#44) carries these columns:
  - `source_id`, `source_row_ref`, and the source's raw place fields, unchanged
  - `source_reference_date`
  - `psgc_version` (`2Q 2026`)
  - `psgc_code` (10 digits; NULL when not matched) and `psgc_name`, so a reviewer sees the matched name next to the raw one
  - `geographic_level` (`reg`, `prov`, `city_mun`, `bgy`)
  - `match_status`: `matched`, `unmatched`, `ambiguous`, `not_in_source` (D-028), or `excluded` (D-031)
  - `match_method`, one of:

    | Value | Meaning | Used by |
    |---|---|---|
    | `code` | The source's code is the PSGC code, and the names agree | Age-group population, HDX, DepEd (EMISD codes) |
    | `code_name_differs` | The source's code is the PSGC code, but the source's name does not find that unit, and the code is accepted only on a stated condition: the name match finds nothing, or it finds another unit while DepEd's barangay name matches the code's barangay and that barangay lies inside the code's city or municipality; a reviewed override that uses the code where the names differ also gets it | DepEd (EMISD codes, #91) |
    | `code_renamed` | The code agrees, and the unit was renamed since the source's version | HDX (4 renamed LGUs) |
    | `code_region_moved` | The code agrees once Sulu's region `19` is read as `09` | HDX, DepEd |
    | `correspondence_code` | The source's old 9-digit code equals PSGC's `Correspondence Code` (D-030) | Poverty, HDX |
    | `name_exact` | The name equals the PSGC name inside the matched parent | DepEd, barangay population, CMCI |
    | `name_old` | The name equals a PSGC `Old names` entry inside the parent | DepEd |
    | `name_normalized` | The names agree after the counted normalization tiers ([shared matching rules](../data/cross-source/README.md#shared-matching-rules)) | DepEd, barangay population, CMCI |
    | `reviewed_alias` | A name or suffix reading decided by review, listed in `config/` with its reason | DepEd (5 aliases), CMCI (16 aliases, 17 suffix readings) |
    | `rollup` | A sub-unit rolled up to its PSGC parent | DepEd (Manila's sub-municipalities) |
    | `spatial` | Matched by geometry, not by name or code | None yet; the 8 Special Geographic Area units in HDX after a spatial check |

  - `population_concept` (`total`, `household`), for population sources only
- **Reason:** One set of columns lets every source's match rate be read the same way, and keeps every reviewed choice visible. The `match_method` list combines the proposal on #74 with @maeveylain's and @saraevcldn's suggestions: `name_in_parent` is split into the three name values, and `manual` is `reviewed_alias`.
- **Consequence:** #44's mapping tables follow this list; a new method is added here first. **Revisit** when the first mapping table is built (#44, #93). Agreed on #74 by @maeveylain and @saraevcldn on 2026-10-09; the combined `match_method` list is new in this entry. `code_name_differs` was added on 2026-10-18 for the #91 rule (PR #153), so `code` keeps meaning that the names agree.
- **Revisited 2026-10-16:** the first map to PSGC, `hdx_adm3_psgc_map` (D-052), carries every column above, with `source_row_ref` = `<adm3_pcode>/<source_reference_date>`, and adds `code_candidate`, `review_status` and `reason`, with the batch and row lineage of both Silver rows. Later maps follow that shape unless their own decision says otherwise.

## D-034: PSA Poverty Stat Silver: unpivot to one row per unit and estimate year

- **Problem:** `psa_poverty_stat` Bronze keeps the workbook's shape (D-018): one `unit` row per city or municipality holds 2018, 2021 and 2023 side by side, each year as five cells (poverty incidence, CV, SE, two 90% limits), with region banner rows between the units. D-021 keeps DepEd's wide shape in Silver, but DepEd's columns are additive counts of one school year, while PSA's five cells per year are one estimate and its precision, and the year is part of what is measured. The values are stored doubles with up to 17 significant digits, some in scientific notation (O-6); 1,073 IDs lost a leading zero (O-2); Kalayaan has no estimates (O-5); CV, SE and the limits carry known statistical warnings (O-7 to O-9).
- **Decision:**
  - Silver unpivots: one row per Bronze `unit` row per estimate year its delivery covers (`estimate_years_covered`), key (`psgc_id`, `estimate_year`), with that year's five measures together. Estimate years come from the contract's columns; a covered year the generated SQL does not know fails the gate (`estimate_years_known`) until the SQL is regenerated. Structural rows (title, header, region banner, blank, footer) stay in Bronze; the gate accounts for them per batch.
  - `psgc_id` is the published `PSGC ID` left-padded to six digits (the unpadded value stays in Bronze), and `correspondence_code` is `psgc_id` + `000` (nine digits; not the ten-digit PSGC code), named like PSGC's own `correspondence_code`, the column Integration joins it to. `region` is the nearest banner above the unit in Excel row order within the same batch and sheet, never inferred from the ID prefix; `municipality` is the published name. The unit rows' sparse province labels and a province code prefix are not carried into Silver: the labels are blank on 1,527 of 1,612 rows and unreliable (O-3), and the 4-digit prefix is shared by two Maguindanao provinces and is not PSGC's province code; the official province comes from PSGC in Integration. No PSGC matching, no name repair, no synthetic rows for places the workbook does not cover: Integration's job.
  - Column names are short and follow the existing Silver tables and the PSGC table they join to (`region`, `municipality`, `correspondence_code`); `estimate_year` keeps its qualifier so it is never confused with DepEd's `school_year`. Of the lineage columns, Silver carries the eight every Silver table carries plus `logical_dataset` and `estimate_years_covered` (PSA's period identity); `source_file`, `source_sheet` and `source_row_kind` are not carried (constant per batch, or always `unit`; recoverable through `batch_id`).
  - Measures are typed as DOUBLE when they match a plain or scientific-notation number exactly (no trimming): the IEEE-754 value Excel stored, with no rounding. A fixed-scale DECIMAL was rejected because it would round small values in scientific notation. The gate compares each Silver value with CAST of its own Bronze cell exactly, with no tolerance. Blank stays NULL, never 0; all five blank is `estimate_status = 'no_estimate'` (kept, not quarantined); some blank is `partial_estimate` (kept, WARN).
  - Statistical warnings are flags, never corrections: `cv_over_20`, `se_cv_inconsistent` (|SE − incidence × CV / 100| > 0.05) and `lower_limit_not_positive`; never NULL. SE is not recomputed and limits are not clipped or replaced.
  - A candidate is quarantined, with every reason, for a blank or malformed ID, a padded ID repeated within an estimate year among current batches (every copy), a measure that is not a number, or an impossible value (incidence outside 0 to 100, negative CV or SE, lower limit above upper, estimate outside its interval). Quarantine above 0 is WARN; above 1% of a batch's units in one estimate year is FAIL (as D-024).
  - Rules live in `config/mappings/psa_poverty_stat.json`; `src/silver/psa_poverty_stat.py` generates the build, the gate and the dictionary, chosen by the file's `generator` key (`src/silver/generators.py`). Build, gate, candidates, publishing and the run record follow D-025 unchanged.
- **Reason:** Each Silver row is then one published estimate with the precision that qualifies it, which is what a planner reads and what Gold's poverty fact needs, and reconciliation is still exact: units × covered years = clean + quarantined, and every value equals its own Bronze cell. Keeping the unpivot in Silver avoids every consumer re-deriving the year from column names. Flagging rather than fixing keeps the publisher's numbers and makes each warning countable.
- **Consequence:** 1,612 clean rows per estimate year for the current workbook (4,836), checked by an independent Python reference on the real workbook and recorded in the evidence; the gate writes 115 results (6 + 7 + 34 × 3), with 9 WARN expected from the flags. A new estimate year (D-018 revision) needs the three Silver files regenerated in the same pull request as the contract change. `estimate_year` is INT (a calendar year, filtered without quotes); the gate compares its text form with Bronze's year columns and `estimate_years_covered`, which stay text. `psgc_id` and `correspondence_code` stay STRING: they are codes, and a number would drop their leading zeros (O-2). Typed as INT by @Catweyine on 2026-10-09, before team review. Cross-year comparability (S-3) is not established by any of this. **Open:** whether `partial_estimate` should quarantine instead (none observed). Relates to D-028 (places with no estimate stay `not_in_source`, nothing imputed) and D-030 (joins through PSGC's Correspondence Code), which this Silver table is built to support. **Provisional:** proposed by @Catweyine on 2026-10-09; the source owner and the team have not yet reviewed it.

## D-040: HDX ADM3 Silver: geometry kept as published text, checked on both engines

- **Problem:** `hdx_boundaries` Bronze keeps each COD-AB ADM3 feature's 31 properties as text and its geometry as the raw GeoJSON text (D-026). D-026 expected Silver to parse the geometry into a spatial type, but DuckDB (local, D-017) and Databricks store a GEOMETRY differently, so a typed column would make the local and Databricks tables differ, and any conversion risks changing the published shape (D-023). `area_sqkm` has up to 14 decimals and `center_lon` up to 14, so the planned DECIMAL(20,10) would round them. The DepEd mapping format (spec.py) knows identifier, free text, category, boolean and count columns, not dates, decimals or geometry.
- **Decision:**
  - The geometry stays the published GeoJSON text in `hdx_adm3_clean`: never simplified, reprojected, or repaired. The build and the gate parse it with `try_to_geometry` (Databricks; `src/ingestion/store.py` gives DuckDB the same name as a macro over its spatial extension, loaded only when a statement uses it). A shape that does not parse, is empty, or is not Polygon/MultiPolygon is quarantined; a shape that parses but is not valid is kept and flagged (`geometry_is_valid`); a published centre outside its own shape is flagged (`center_inside_geometry`). Geometry types are compared as `upper(replace(.., 'ST_', ''))`, since Databricks says `ST_Polygon` where DuckDB says `POLYGON`; the centre point is built from the published text as GeoJSON too, so both shapes carry the same SRID.
  - `area_sqkm` is DECIMAL(20,14) and `center_lat`/`center_lon` DECIMAL(18,14), typed only when the published text fits the type exactly (a generated pattern of at most p−s integer and s decimal digits), so no digit is rounded; anything else quarantines the row. `valid_on`/`valid_to` are DATE when they are `YYYY-MM-DD`. P-codes and parent codes stay exactly as published; a broken adm3 → adm2 → adm1 chain is flagged (`hierarchy_consistent`), not quarantined. Names are whitespace-normalized only; the four category columns (region, country, version, language) are gated against reviewed labels. The 15 always-blank alternate-name and language columns are kept.
  - A repeated `adm3_pcode` within a reference date quarantines every copy; quarantine above 0 is WARN, above 1% of a batch FAIL (as D-024). Build, gate, candidates, publishing and the run record follow D-025 unchanged.
  - Rules live in `config/mappings/hdx_boundaries.json`; `src/silver/hdx_boundaries.py` generates the build, the gate and the dictionary, chosen by the file's `generator` key (`src/silver/generators.py`, as D-034).
- **Reason:** Text is the same value on both engines and is exactly what COD-AB published, and every shape is still checked on every run with the spatial functions. Exact DECIMALs keep every published digit while giving Gold numbers to compute with. Keeping the PSGC question out of Silver follows the cross-source finding that COD-AB reuses some PSGC code numbers for different units (#74, HB-2).

## D-043: Monitoring: run emails, source freshness, and data-quality alerts

- **Problem:** A run can fail, hang on Free Edition capacity, or succeed while a check recorded FAIL or a source has not been refreshed in years. Job success says only that tasks ran. Without alerts the team learns about any of these only by opening the job. The repository is public, so teammates' email addresses should not be committed.
- **Decision:**
  - The job emails whoever deployed it (`${workspace.current_user.userName}`) when a run fails, timeouts included, or is still going after 45 minutes. Runs stop and fail after 90 minutes. Cancelled and skipped runs are not reported.
  - `90_validate_freshness` runs after the source-lane fan-in, passed or not, and records one `source_fresh` result per loaded source. A source whose latest succeeded batch in `current_batches` is older than its limit, or which has none, is WARN, never FAIL: its data is still correct, only possibly out of date. Age counts from when we loaded the batch, the one date every source has; only `psa_psgc` records a publication date. Limits and their basis live in `config/freshness.yml`; the SQL is generated from it and `databricks.yml`.
  - Two SQL alerts evaluate daily at 08:00 Asia/Manila and email the deployer, listing the rows: `quality_failures` (the latest run's FAILs, newest result per check, so a check rerun in a repaired run replaces its FAIL) and `stale_sources` (the WARNs of the latest freshness check).
  - Thresholds are bundle variables and `freshness_as_of` is a job parameter, so each signal can be tested once without changing code or data.
- **Reason:** The deployer is the person who changed something, so they hear about it first, and nothing personal enters Git. The duration thresholds come from observed runs: whole runs took 6 min 43 s to 18 min 22 s, the first run 39 minutes, 35.5 of them waiting for capacity (D-017). Forty-five minutes is above all of them, and 90 leaves room for a full Bronze-to-Gold run. A stale source as WARN keeps a slow publisher from failing every run, which would teach the team to ignore failures. A daily alert costs one short warehouse query; the job has no schedule, so a result waits at most a day. The job's failure email arrives at once.
- **Consequence:** On `dev`, only the person who deployed is told, and development mode pauses the alerts' schedules, so they are evaluated by hand there. `prod` (#99) must override the recipients so the whole team is told. A pull request that adds a source load must add its freshness limit, or `tests/test_freshness.py` fails. Each source owner confirms their limit. Because age counts from our load, a new release can be out for up to its limit before it warns: PSGC 2Q 2026 was published 2026-06-30 and loaded 2026-10-08, so a 3Q release would warn only in February 2027. Counting PSGC from its publication date instead would keep it WARN every day while D-012 keeps 2Q 2026 as the master reference; revisit if that pin is lifted (raised by @maeveylain in review). `quality_failures` reads only the latest run, so a partial run after a failing one hides its FAILs until the next full run. The alerts count their own rows instead of aggregating, because an aggregation makes the email show only the count. Every signal fired once on `dev` on 2026-10-13 ([evidence](../../evidence/pipeline-runs/2026-10-13-monitoring-alerts.md)). **Provisional:** proposed by @hyenalouise on 2026-10-13 in #100.

## D-047: Question v1 metric counting rules

- **Problem:** The draft [metric specification](../business/metric-specification.md) left the education levels and the AP1 to AP3 counting rules open, so the data model and Analytics could not build the measures the same way.
- **Decision:**
  - **Levels:** ES is Kindergarten, Grades 1–6, and ES non-graded; JHS is Grades 7–10 and JHS non-graded; SHS is Grades 11–12. Non-graded learners count in their level: 82,347 public-school learners in SY 2023-24.
  - **AP1 schools:** as refined by D-050, a school is active when at least one offered level has a complete `learner_count` above 0 in that school year. Schools with no complete positive offered level are kept and shown in coverage counts but are not counted and do not serve a barangay (23 public schools in SY 2020-21, 4 in SY 2023-24, 1 in SY 2024-25, and 3 in SY 2025-26). An incomplete offered level does not make the school inactive when another offered level has a complete positive count. Annexes count as schools, labelled "public schools (including annexes)" with the annex count shown.
  - **AP2 classrooms:** learners per reported instructional room is total learners divided by total instructional rooms, per school per school year, across all levels. Total rooms are the sum of the ES, JHS, and SHS counts that are not blank; a school is usable when the total is above 0; all three blank is missing, not zero (608 public schools in SY 2023-24).
  - **AP3 teachers:** the ratio is calculated by level (ES, JHS, SHS). Teachers are Teacher I–III, Master Teacher I–IV, Head Teacher I–VI, SPED/SNED teachers, Special Science teachers, Instructors, and locally funded teachers (SEF province, SEF city/municipality, LGU, other funding), with Kindergarten locally funded teachers under ES. Principals and all non-teaching staff are excluded, giving 935,421 public-school teachers in SY 2023-24. The four funding columns are shown per level. Schools with learners at a level but teachers only at another level are flagged (255 at JHS, 109 at SHS). The source cannot show whether one person is counted in two columns.
  - **AP3 annexes:** with no mother-school link, AP3 is labelled project-defined. An annex that reports 0 teachers keeps its learners in the city or municipality total instead of being dropped by the usable-denominator rule (48 annexes, 21,114 learners in SY 2023-24).
- **Reason:** Non-graded learners are enrolled and use classrooms and teachers, so leaving them out would undercount demand at ES and JHS. DepEd publishes no mother-school ID (#134), and 52 annexes are the only public elementary school in their barangay, so AP1 must count annexes; the Technical Notes annex rule (p. 8) applies only to the teacher–learner ratio. Rooms are shared across levels (768 public schools have JHS learners and ES rooms but no JHS rooms), totals follow the Technical Notes classroom–learner formula (p. 8), and learner coverage is 99.3% with totals against 98.8% by level. The Technical Notes define the teacher–learner ratio at a specific level, so AP3 keeps levels apart. Summed to the city or municipality, keeping zero-teacher annexes' learners matches DepEd's add-to-mother rule whenever the annex and its mother school are in the same city or municipality.
- **Consequence:** Every level-specific measure uses the same column sets, and AP1 to AP3 can be built from these rules. AP3 is never presented as DepEd's teacher–learner ratio. D-050 resolves how a multi-level school counts in AP1 level-specific school counts and how schools without a unique barangay match are handled; the full rules are in the metric specification. **Provisional:** proposed by @hyenalouise on 2026-10-16 in #129; the team has not yet reviewed it. Updated on 2026-10-18 for #66: D-050 refines the active-school rule to use any offered level with a complete positive learner count; the AP1 scope and annex treatment are unchanged.

## D-057: LGU priority rule, version 1

- **Problem:** The panel (2026-10-18) found no clear answer to the business question and asked for a ranking of the cities and municipalities where the school, classroom, and teacher measures flag at the same time. The [metric specification](../business/metric-specification.md) kept classroom and teacher priority in separate views with no combined ranking, D-042 treated AP1 as supporting context, and peer groups, the planned comparison, were parked on mentor advice the same day (#140 and #148 closed). The priority label in the dashboard contract stayed blocked until a ranking rule was approved (#142).
- **Decision:**
  - **User and scope:** the user is LGU Local School Boards (the team confirmed this on 2026-10-19; D-042 named DepEd Schools Division Offices as well). The result is SY 2023-24, the only year in which enrollment, facilities, and personnel all match by `school_id`; public, non-overseas schools; one row per city or municipality. A school counts toward an LGU only through the reviewed school-to-PSGC match (D-049). Special Geographic Area schools enter results the same way, when their DepEd code resolves to one of the eight SGA municipalities (D-049). Barangays and schools are drill-down evidence and are never ranked.
  - **Measures:** AP1 learners per public school (population per public school, part of D-042's AP1, is shown beside it as context and does not count toward the tier), AP2 learners per reported instructional room (all levels, D-047), and AP3 learners per teacher by level, each calculated as in the metric specification (sums before division, a blank is missing, coverage shown).
  - **Coverage gate:** an LGU is ranked on a measure only when its coverage for that measure is at least 90%. For AP2 and for each AP3 level this is the published learner coverage, the share of learners in schools with a usable value. For AP1 it is the share of active public schools whose all-level enrollment is complete, because the learners of an incomplete school cannot be counted. AP3 is high when its re-ranked percentile (below) is high using the levels that pass; it is not high only when every offered level passes and it is still not high; otherwise it is not assessed, so a level without enough data is never read as not high. An LGU gets a tier only when all three measures are assessed; otherwise its tier is **Data review** and the measures it could compute are still shown.
  - **Comparison and high line:** each measure is ranked with `PERCENT_RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY lgu_type ORDER BY ratio)`, (rank − 1) ÷ (n − 1), among the LGUs of the same type (city or municipality) that pass its coverage gate. Tied values share the lowest rank, and a higher ratio means more pressure on every measure. A measure is **high** at 0.90 or above. AP3 is ranked in two steps: each level is ranked among the LGUs of the type that offer it and pass its gate; then each LGU's highest level percentile is ranked again with `PERCENT_RANK()` within type, among the LGUs with at least one passing level, and AP3 is high when that second percentile is 0.90 or above. The result names the level with the highest percentile (on a tie, the lower level).
  - **Tiers:** **Critical** (all 3 high), **Elevated** (2), **Targeted** (1, naming the measure), **Monitor** (0), and **Data review** (a measure not assessed). A count of high measures is not a weighted score: each measure is still shown with its value, percentile, and coverage. "Critical" is always shown with its meaning, high on all three measures compared with LGUs of the same type, because it is relative pressure, not a shortage. The priority table stores a fixed tier code (3, 2, 1, 0, `data_review`) beside the label, so a renamed label does not change the query.
  - **Order within a tier:** affected learners first (public-school learners in the LGU in SY 2023-24, the AP1 enrollment total), highest first; then the lowest of the three percentiles, highest first. The order never moves an LGU between tiers.
  - **Stable:** the same rule is also run against all LGUs nationally, without the type partition. An LGU that is Critical both within its type and nationally is marked Stable. The tier always comes from the within-type ranking.
  - **Checks beside the ranking, never changing a tier:** the LGUs that join Critical at high lines of 0.85 and 0.80 (a lower line only adds LGUs); the share of the Critical list kept, and the LGUs that join, with a 95% coverage gate; the national comparison (the Stable flag); the LGUs whose tier would change without outlier schools; the share of LGUs high on each measure by type (about 10% each is expected); and the overlap of the measures, as the Spearman rank correlation between AP1 to AP3 by type and the share of LGUs high on one measure that are also high on another, beside the 10% expected if they were unrelated. An outlier school is above Q3 + 3 × IQR of its school-level ratio, with the quartiles computed within LGU type over the public schools with a usable denominator in SY 2023-24: learners per room (all levels) for AP2 and learners per teacher per level for AP3. Outlier schools stay in the published totals (metric specification rule 8), and the flagged list is saved with each run. No significance test is used: the sources cover every public school, not a sample, so there is no sampling error to test; reporting error remains, and the coverage gate and the outlier check address it.
  - **Revisit trigger:** the rule is revisited when, under any check, fewer than 80% of the Critical LGUs stay Critical and at least 3 leave. The 80% is the team's choice, not a published standard; the count keeps one city from tripping it on a short list.
  - **Context, never changing a tier:** **Similar LGUs** replaces peer groups on the LGU profile as a list of the most similar LGUs of the same type. The planned inputs are income class, population density (population ÷ `hdx_boundaries.area_sqkm`), poverty incidence, and population. The method is not part of this rule: how inputs are standardized, how income class is encoded, the distance measure, how many LGUs are listed, the rule for a missing input (44 areas have no PSA poverty row and the 8 Special Geographic Area municipalities have no HDX area), and the tie-break are set and recorded in #96 before the view is built. The DepEd Order No. 40 screen (barangays without a matched public elementary school) is shown beside AP1 as its official benchmark (D-045). AP4 has no SY 2023-24 value and is context once it is built.
  - **Wording:** the result is relative capacity pressure, never a shortage, a deficit, or a number of rooms or teachers needed (D-045).
- **Reason:**
  - The method follows CDC/ATSDR's Social Vulnerability Index, which percentile-ranks each area on each variable, flags the areas at the 90th percentile or above, and counts the flags ([SVI 2016 documentation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/media/pdfs/2024/08/SVI2016Documentation_01192022.pdf)). The 90th percentile was also the per-indicator line of the US Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool ([USGS summary](https://sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/66159c34d34e7eb9eb7d5550)), while EPA's EJScreen uses the 80th percentile only as a screen for further review ([EPA](https://19january2025snapshot.epa.gov/ejscreen/how-interpret-ejscreen-data)). The 90th is the list to act on first; the 85th and 80th are reported as checks.
  - AP3 is re-ranked so that each measure has about the same chance of being high, which a count of high measures needs to have no hidden weight. Calling AP3 high when any level is high gives teachers up to three chances at the 90th percentile, 1 − 0.9³ ≈ 27% if the levels were independent. On the v4 prototype it flagged 20% of cities and of municipalities against 10% for AP1 and AP2, and AP3 was one of the high measures for 96% of municipalities with two or more; re-ranking brings it to 9–10%. Equal weights on paper do not give equal influence ([Becker et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2017.03.056)), and the SVI gives each variable one flag. This replaces the team's 2026-10-18 choice (high if any level is high) after the #190 review; the level is still named, so one stretched level stays visible.
  - A count of high measures puts the three resources on one list without weights, which the panel asked for and the metric specification ruled out only for weighted scores.
  - NCES sets a 95% target for collections that cover a whole population, recommends a bias check below 90%, and requires one below 85% ([NCES Statistical Standards, 2002 edition, Standard 2-2](https://nces.ed.gov/statprog/2002/std2_2.asp): 2-2-1, Guideline 2-2-1A, 2-2-4). The 90% gate is the point at which NCES would not ask for an extra check. NCES writes these rules for response rates and we apply them to learner coverage, so this is an adaptation; the 95% result is reported as a check.
  - Ordering by affected learners keeps very small LGUs from leading a tier only because their ratios vary more: in an early prototype, which compared within peer groups and combined all teacher levels, ordering by percentile put Jomalig, with about 2,300 learners, second. Small units land at both extremes because small numbers vary more ([Wainer 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/peter/498.Sp16/Equation.pdf)).
  - CDC's SVI publishes a national and a within-state ranking for the same areas and notes that a score means something different in each ([SVI FAQ](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/php/svi/svi-frequently-asked-questions-faqs.html)); the Stable flag shows which LGUs do not depend on the comparison choice. The other checks follow the OECD and JRC advice to test a ranking against its method choices ([Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/jrc47008_handbook_final.pdf); [Saisana, Saltelli and Tarantola 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-985X.2005.00350.x)). JRC audits of published indices report robustness as the share of units that keep their place across method choices; the revisit trigger applies the same idea to the Critical list.
  - The outlier fence is Tukey's outer fence for extreme values ([NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook 7.1.6](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section1/prc16.htm)). Its quartiles are computed within LGU type because the tier compares within type, and statistical agencies check ratios within comparable groups (Hidiroglou and Berthelot 1986, *Survey Methodology* 12:73–83, the ratio check the [US Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2012/adrm/rrs2012-10.pdf) uses in its annual business surveys). On the v4 prototype, one national fence (88 learners per room) flagged 4.5% of city schools and 2.4% of municipal ones, because city schools have more learners per room (median 32.6 against 26.0); fences within type flag 2.4% and 2.8%.
  - The three ratios share learners as the numerator, so some correlation between them is built in ([Pearson 1897](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspl.1896.0076)). The overlap check shows how far Critical means pressure on all three resources at once; on the v4 prototype, 62% of municipalities high on AP2 were also high on AP3.
